# Art Direction

Read this when composing or prompting a Starryear-Ink artwork.

## Canvas and physical object

- 1024 × 1536 px, RGB, vertical 2:3.
- One flat authored sheet with no outer poster frame.
- Warm matte ivory handmade paper: fine fibers, restrained mottling, tiny specks, shallow wrinkles, soft absorbency.
- Avoid pure white, strong yellow-brown parchment, heavy distressing, uniform digital grain, mockup shadows, and glossy finish.

## Evidence band

- Direct source crop, normally 26–35% of canvas height; 30% is a useful starting point.
- Full-width unless a source-specific offset preserves evidence better.
- One irregular continuous hand-torn lower edge with pulp strands and a shallow physical shadow.
- A partial handmade-paper embrace may rise around lower corners or side margins. It must remain irregular and preserve at least 90% of important evidence.
- One to three local seam events may cross the boundary: a color stain entering fibers, a halftone fragment dissolving across the tear, or an upward ink gesture touching a nonessential photo margin.

## Printed-memory material

The printed memory is analytical, dry, incomplete, and spatially impossible.

Use three to seven source-derived fragments selected from:

- partial silhouettes and isolated characteristic edges;
- repeated intervals encoded as nodes, voids, bars, or fading echoes;
- negative-space contours;
- perspective traces and enlarged axes;
- detached shadows or reflections;
- incomplete halftone fields;
- transfer-print loss and screen-print residue;
- etching-like line, graphite residue, and broken registration;
- small reduced-color fragments or photographic residue.

Fragments may change scale, invert locally, drift from expected positions, cross impossible planes, become transparent, lose sections, or merge with negative space. Preserve structural logic, not complete objects.

## Ink-afterimage material

The ink state is emotional, wet, expanded, low-frequency, and atmospheric.

- Two to five principal transparent pigment masses.
- One to four necessary gestures or residual contours.
- Capillary feathering, cauliflower blooms, granular sediment, tide marks, broken dry brush, or diluted mineral wash.
- At least half the region remains untouched or nearly untouched paper.
- Preserve the scene through mass, spacing, direction, and rhythm rather than detailed silhouettes.

Do not let the afterimage sit as a decorative vignette on the bottom edge. Give one mass upward directional energy inherited from the source.

## Color

- Extract two or three useful source color families.
- One hue leads; one or two remain quieter supporters.
- Convert them to transparent granulating ink instead of flat digital fills.
- Keep saturated color subordinate and black / near-black below about 20% of the lower pigment area.
- Avoid muddy mixtures, rainbow competition, decorative seal red, and a lower field made only from grey because it feels safe.

## Interpenetration

The printed memory and ink afterimage overlap without losing their material difference.

- A wash can narrow into dry brush and collect halftone.
- Dots can suspend inside translucent pigment.
- An etched contour can disappear beneath a bloom.
- Printed fragments can descend, fragment, and become pigment deposits.
- A source axis can start as a thin printed rule and terminate as a broad wet gesture.
- A negative-space opening can enlarge into the principal blank paper field.

There must be no exact horizontal line where print ends and ink begins.

## Controlled surrealism

Transform the behavior of real evidence. Good operations include scale contradiction, displacement, detached reflection, folded perspective, continuation beyond physical limits, impossible overlap, partial inversion, shadow separation, spatial compression, and material migration.

Do not invent moons, planets, portals, floating islands, generic dream figures, unrelated birds, fantasy architecture, decorative clouds, mystical symbols, or scenery absent from the source.

## Editorial notation

Notation is optional and subordinate. If used, create one compact relationship tied to a real source axis or tension: a small crosshair, three slightly unequal micro-dots, and one short interrupted rule. At most one mark may inherit the accent hue. Do not stamp the same coordinates on every artwork.

Avoid fake archive copy. If an internal identifier is specifically desired and can be added exactly, use one microscopic `SY·E→M·25` inside necessary notation. Omit it when exact text rendering is unreliable.

## Text

Default to no title. User-provided wording must be exact. Observational microcopy may be used only when it genuinely helps and can be added deterministically. Use a tiny serif or typewriter-like face; never use large headlines, brush calligraphy, seals, slogans, pseudo-English, or garbled text.

Add one exact, small `Starryear` signature in a quiet lower margin for public work.
